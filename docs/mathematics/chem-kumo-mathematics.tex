\section{Scope and conventions}
KUMO is a non-SCF evaluator of real ground-state electronic fields from
Gaussian formatted checkpoint (FCHK) data. It neither determines the electronic
state nor relaxes orbital coefficients. All derivatives below are with respect
to the electronic Cartesian coordinate $\mathbf r$, with the molecular geometry
and FCHK data fixed. No nuclear derivatives or electronic response equations
are implemented. Atomic units are used: distances in bohr, energies in hartree.
AO and MO values have dimensions $a_0^{-3/2}$, density $a_0^{-3}$,
its gradient $a_0^{-4}$, Hessian and Laplacian $a_0^{-5}$, and the positive
kinetic-energy density $E_h a_0^{-3}$ in atomic units.

\section{Gaussian atomic-orbital basis}
A Cartesian primitive centered at $\mathbf A$ is
\begin{equation}
 g_{\ell mn}(\mathbf r;\alpha)=N_{\ell mn}(\alpha)
 u^\ell v^m w^n\exp[-\alpha(u^2+v^2+w^2)],\qquad
 (u,v,w)=\mathbf r-\mathbf A.
\end{equation}
An AO is a contracted linear combination of primitives (with the appropriate
normalization and contraction conventions):
\begin{equation}
 \chi_\mu(\mathbf r)=\sum_p d_{\mu p}g_{\mu p}(\mathbf r).
\end{equation}
For pure spherical shells, a fixed Cartesian-to-real-spherical linear
transformation maps primitive or contracted Cartesian components into the
FCHK AO order. This same transformation is applied to all spatial derivatives.
For combined SP shells, S and P contractions use their respective coefficients.
The FCHK record for SP contraction coefficients need not exist when no SP
shell is present. The AO ordering and normalization must agree with the
coefficient and density matrices read from the same FCHK.

\section{Analytic AO derivatives through second order}
Define the polynomial factors
\begin{align}
 f_\ell(u)&=u^\ell,\\
 D_\ell(u;\alpha)&=\ell u^{\ell-1}-2\alpha u^{\ell+1},\\
 E_\ell(u;\alpha)&=\ell(\ell-1)u^{\ell-2}
 -2\alpha(2\ell+1)u^\ell+4\alpha^2u^{\ell+2}.
\end{align}
Terms whose prefactors vanish are evaluated as zero, without forming
negative powers at the origin. With $G=N_{\ell mn}\exp[-\alpha(u^2+v^2+w^2)]$,
\begin{align}
 \partial_x g_{\ell mn}&=G D_\ell(u;\alpha)f_m(v)f_n(w),\\
 \partial_{xx}g_{\ell mn}&=G E_\ell(u;\alpha)f_m(v)f_n(w),\\
 \partial_{xy}g_{\ell mn}&=G D_\ell(u;\alpha)D_m(v;\alpha)f_n(w).
\end{align}
The other Cartesian components follow by permutation. Derivation commutes
with contraction and the Cartesian-to-pure transformation. The AO evaluator
returns $\chi_\mu$, three first derivatives, and six unique second derivatives
in the order $(xx,yy,zz,xy,xz,yz)$. This is the storage convention, not the
matrix layout of the symmetric $3\times3$ Hessian.

\section{Molecular orbitals}
For real molecular orbitals in spin channel $\sigma\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$,
\begin{align}
 \psi_i^\sigma(\mathbf r)&=\sum_\mu C_{\mu i}^\sigma\chi_\mu(\mathbf r),\\
 \partial_a\psi_i^\sigma&=\sum_\mu C_{\mu i}^\sigma\partial_a\chi_\mu,\\
 \partial_{ab}\psi_i^\sigma&=\sum_\mu C_{\mu i}^\sigma\partial_{ab}\chi_\mu.
\end{align}
These are derivatives of the signed orbital amplitude, not of its square.
The public MO indices are one-based, whereas internal coefficient matrices
use zero-based indexing. Alpha and beta select orbital channels, not total
or spin-difference orbitals. No MO phase alignment is applied.

\section{Spin densities and the AO density matrix}
Let $\boldsymbol\chi(\mathbf r)$ be the column vector of AO values and
$P^\sigma$ a real symmetric spin density matrix. Then
\begin{equation}
 \rho_\sigma=\boldsymbol\chi^T P^\sigma\boldsymbol\chi
 =\sum_i n_{i\sigma}(\psi_i^\sigma)^2,
 \qquad P^\sigma=C^\sigma n^\sigma(C^\sigma)^T.
\end{equation}
Here $n^\sigma$ is diagonal and contains spin-orbital occupations. In the
restricted closed-shell case the two spin matrices are equal and each
contains half the total density. Define
\begin{equation}
 P^{\mathrm{total}}=P^\alpha+P^\beta,\qquad
 P^{\mathrm{spin}}=P^\alpha-P^\beta.
\end{equation}
KUMO supports total, alpha, beta, and alpha-minus-beta components.
The spin-difference field is signed and need not be nonnegative.

\section{Spatial derivatives of the electron density}
Write $\boldsymbol\chi_a=\partial_a\boldsymbol\chi$ and
$\boldsymbol\chi_{ab}=\partial_a\partial_b\boldsymbol\chi$.
For any of the symmetric matrices $P^s$ described above,
\begin{align}
 \rho_s&=\boldsymbol\chi^T P^s\boldsymbol\chi,\\
 \partial_a\rho_s&=2\boldsymbol\chi_a^T P^s\boldsymbol\chi,\\
 \partial_{ab}\rho_s&=2\boldsymbol\chi_{ab}^T P^s\boldsymbol\chi
       +2\boldsymbol\chi_a^T P^s\boldsymbol\chi_b,\\
 \nabla^2\rho_s&=\sum_{a=x,y,z}\partial_{aa}\rho_s
                  =\operatorname{tr}H(\rho_s).
\end{align}
The full Hessian is symmetric; the off-diagonal components are evaluated
analytically. The positive-definite kinetic-energy density for a physical
spin channel is
\begin{equation}
 \tau_\sigma(\mathbf r)=\frac12\sum_{a=x,y,z}
 \boldsymbol\chi_a^T P^\sigma\boldsymbol\chi_a
 =\frac12\sum_i n_{i\sigma}|\nabla\psi_i^\sigma|^2.
\end{equation}
The same linear formula applied to $P^{\mathrm{spin}}$ yields
$\tau_\alpha-\tau_\beta$, which is not necessarily positive.
The identity
\begin{equation}
 \nabla^2\rho_s=2(\nabla^2\boldsymbol\chi)^TP^s\boldsymbol\chi
 +4\tau_s
\end{equation}
provides an additional algebraic consistency check.

\section{Blocked dense contractions with Eigen}
For $B$ spatial points, let $\Phi,\Phi_x,\Phi_y,\Phi_z$ be
$N_{\mathrm{AO}}\times B$ panels of AO values and first derivatives.
The implementation packs these into one column-major matrix
\begin{equation}
 A=[\Phi\ \Phi_x\ \Phi_y\ \Phi_z]
 \in\mathbb R^{N_{\mathrm{AO}}\times4B}
\end{equation}
and applies the selected density matrix in one Eigen matrix product:
\begin{equation}
 V=P^s A=[V_0\ V_x\ V_y\ V_z].
\end{equation}
For each point (column) $j$, the fields are obtained by vector products:
\begin{align}
 \rho_{s,j}&=\Phi_j^T(V_0)_j,\\
 (\nabla\rho_s)_{a,j}&=2(\Phi_a)_j^T(V_0)_j,\\
 (H_s)_{ab,j}&=2(\Phi_{ab})_j^T(V_0)_j
                  +2(\Phi_a)_j^T(V_b)_j,\\
 \tau_{s,j}&=\tfrac12\sum_a(\Phi_a)_j^T(V_a)_j.
\end{align}
Six second-derivative panels are retained for the linear reductions but
\emph{are not} multiplied by $P^s$. Only the diagonal pointwise contractions
are required; no $B\times B$ field matrix is formed. The AO derivatives
are still evaluated point by point inside each block.

The dense matrix product requires $O(4N_{\mathrm{AO}}^2B)$ arithmetic
per block; AO derivative evaluation and reductions scale approximately
linearly with $N_{\mathrm{AO}}$ for fixed basis structure. The fused product
reduces separate Eigen calls and may improve reuse of $P^s$ in cache,
but it does not change the asymptotic complexity or reduce the number of
mathematical density-matrix applications. Section~\ref{sec:screening}
describes how negligible AOs are left out of each block.

For a full block, ten AO panels (one value, three gradients, six Hessians)
and four product panels together require approximately
$14N_{\mathrm{AO}}B$ doubles, excluding the density matrices, output and
temporary storage. At $N_{\mathrm{AO}}=1410$, $B=128$, this is approximately
20.2 MB of panel data. The public blocked API returns a vector of results,
so its total output memory grows with the number of points even though AO
scratch data are block-local.

\section{AO screening and the shell cutoff}\label{sec:screening}
Gaussian AOs decay quickly, so in a block of nearby grid points most of them
are negligible at every point. For a block, an AO is \emph{screened} when
its value, and for derivative requests all its first and second derivatives,
are at most a threshold $\varepsilon_s$ (default $10^{-12}$) in absolute
value at every point of the block. Screened AOs are removed from the panels
and from the rows and columns of $P^s$ (or from the rows of the occupied MO
coefficients in the AO--MO contraction), which replaces the
$N_{\mathrm{AO}}\times N_{\mathrm{AO}}$ product by a much smaller one. The
change in the density is at rounding level in the tested systems; a
threshold of zero gives the exact contraction.

Independently, each shell has a radius $r_c$ beyond which a rigorous bound
on every AO of the shell and on its first and second derivatives is at most
$10^{-14}$. For a primitive of exponent $a$ and Cartesian degree $\ell$,
every factor of the value and derivative kernels is bounded for $r\ge1$ by
a multiple of $r^{\ell+2}e^{-ar^2}$, so
\begin{equation}
 |\chi|,\ |\partial\chi|,\ |\partial^2\chi|\le
 T\sum_p W_p C_p\, r^{\ell+2}e^{-a_pr^2},\qquad
 C_p=1+\ell+\ell^2+2a_p(2\ell+2)+4a_p^2,
\end{equation}
with $W_p$ the largest contraction weight of primitive $p$ and $T$ the
largest absolute row sum of the pure transform (1 for Cartesian shells).
Each term decreases beyond $\sqrt{(\ell+2)/(2a_p)}$, and $r_c$ is found by
bisection. At points farther than $r_c$ the shell's AOs are returned as
exact zeros.

\section{Derived density descriptors}
With $\tau_\sigma$ as above, the Pauli kinetic-energy density of a spin
channel and its uniform-gas reference are
\begin{equation}
 D_\sigma=\tau_\sigma-\frac{|\nabla\rho_\sigma|^2}{8\rho_\sigma},\qquad
 D^0_\sigma=\frac{3}{10}(6\pi^2)^{2/3}\rho_\sigma^{5/3},
\end{equation}
and the electron localization function (Becke and Edgecombe) is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{ELF}=\frac{1}{1+(D/D^0)^2}.
\end{equation}
For a spin channel $D$ and $D^0$ are those of the channel; for the total ELF
they are summed over $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (spin-polarized form), which for a
closed shell reduces to $D=\tau-|\nabla\rho|^2/(8\rho)$ and
$D^0=\frac{3}{10}(3\pi^2)^{2/3}\rho^{5/3}$. Channels without density are
skipped, and $\mathrm{ELF}=0$ where there is no density. The Pauli term
equals the cancellation-free orbital (Lagrange) form
\begin{equation}
 D_\sigma=\frac{1}{2\rho_\sigma}\sum_{i<j}
 n_in_j\,|\psi_i\nabla\psi_j-\psi_j\nabla\psi_i|^2,
\end{equation}
which the tests use as an independent check.

The reduced density gradient of the total density is
\begin{equation}
 s=\frac{|\nabla\rho|}{2(3\pi^2)^{1/3}\rho^{4/3}},
\end{equation}
reported as at most 100 (also where $\rho\le0$, where it is undefined), so
that Cube files stay finite. For the non-covalent interaction analysis,
$\operatorname{sign}(\lambda_2)\rho$ uses the middle eigenvalue
$\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3$ of the density Hessian, with
$\lambda_2=0$ counted as positive.

\section{Molecular electrostatic potential}
The electrostatic potential, in hartree per elementary charge, is
\begin{equation}
 V(\mathbf C)=\sum_A\frac{Z_A}{|\mathbf C-\mathbf R_A|}
 -\sum_{\mu\nu}P_{\mu\nu}\int\frac{\chi_\mu(\mathbf r)\chi_\nu(\mathbf r)}
 {|\mathbf r-\mathbf C|}\,d\mathbf r,
\end{equation}
with the nuclear charges $Z_A$ of the FCHK (effective charges with ECPs)
and the total density matrix $P$. At a point within $10^{-8}$ bohr of a
nucleus that nucleus's singular term is omitted, which gives the potential
at the nucleus due to the electrons and the other nuclei.

\subsection{Hermite expansion}
Pure shells are handled by transforming $P$ to Cartesian components. The
product of Cartesian primitives with exponents $\alpha,\beta$ at
$\mathbf A,\mathbf B$ is a Gaussian at
$\mathbf P=(\alpha\mathbf A+\beta\mathbf B)/p$, $p=\alpha+\beta$, times
$K=\exp(-\alpha\beta|\mathbf A-\mathbf B|^2/p)$, expanded in Hermite
Gaussians $\Lambda_{tuv}=\partial^t_{P_x}\partial^u_{P_y}\partial^v_{P_z}
e^{-p|\mathbf r-\mathbf P|^2}$ with the McMurchie--Davidson coefficients
\begin{equation}
 E^{i+1,j}_t=\frac{1}{2p}E^{ij}_{t-1}+X_{PA}E^{ij}_t+(t+1)E^{ij}_{t+1},
 \qquad E^{00}_0=1,
\end{equation}
and likewise for $j$ with $X_{PB}$. Contracting $P$ with these coefficients
once per evaluation gives, for every primitive pair, coefficients $c_{tuv}$
such that its contribution to the electronic potential is
$-\sum_{tuv}c_{tuv}R_{tuv}(p,\mathbf P-\mathbf C)$, with
\begin{align}
 R^n_{000}&=(-2p)^nF_n(p|\mathbf P-\mathbf C|^2),\\
 R^n_{t+1,u,v}&=t\,R^{n+1}_{t-1,u,v}+X_{PC}R^{n+1}_{tuv}
\end{align}
(and likewise for $u$, $v$), $R_{tuv}=R^0_{tuv}$; the factor $2\pi/p$ of
$\int\Lambda_{tuv}/|\mathbf r-\mathbf C|=\frac{2\pi}{p}R_{tuv}$ is included
in $c_{tuv}$.

\subsection{Boys function}
$F_m(T)=\int_0^1t^{2m}e^{-Tt^2}dt$ for $m\le32$. For $T<40$ the highest
order needed comes from a seventh-order Taylor expansion about the nearest
point of a table with spacing 0.05, using $dF_m/dT=-F_{m+1}$; the table is
built from the positive series
$F_M(T)=e^{-T}\sum_k(2T)^k/[(2M+1)(2M+3)\cdots(2M+2k+1)]$. Lower orders
follow from the stable downward recursion
$F_{m-1}=(2TF_m+e^{-T})/(2m-1)$. For $T\ge40$,
$F_0=\frac12\sqrt{\pi/T}$ (the error function differs from 1 by less than
$10^{-18}$) and the upward recursion
$F_{m+1}=[(2m+1)F_m-e^{-T}]/(2T)$ is stable. The result agrees with
quadrature of the definition to $2.3\times10^{-15}$ relative.

\subsection{Far field}
The Evaluator processes the grid in clusters of $8\times8\times8$ points,
with centre $\mathbf b$ and radius $\rho$. The potential of one pair splits
into a point-multipole part and an erfc part:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_kc_kR_{\alpha_k}(p,\mathbf P-\mathbf C)
 =\sum_ke_k\,\partial^{\alpha_k}\frac1R
 -\sum_ke_k\,\partial^{\alpha_k}\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt pR)}{R},
 \qquad e_k=\frac{c_k}{2}\sqrt{\frac\pi p},
\end{equation}
with $R=|\mathbf P-\mathbf C|$. Two bounds are used. Every derivative of
order $m$ of $1/|\mathbf x|$, as a symmetric multilinear form, has norm at
most $m!/|\mathbf x|^{m+1}$ (Legendre polynomials are bounded by 1, and by
Banach's theorem the norm of a symmetric form on a Hilbert space equals that
of its diagonal). Every derivative of order $m$ of
$\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt pR)/R=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\sqrt p}^\infty
e^{-s^2R^2}ds$ at $R\ge r$ is bounded, through Cram\'er's inequality
$|H_n(x)|e^{-x^2/2}\le k\sqrt{2^nn!}$ ($k<1.0865$) for each Cartesian
factor, by
\begin{equation}
 G_m(r)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}k^3\sqrt{2^mm!}
 \int_{\sqrt p}^\infty s^me^{-s^2r^2/2}ds,
\end{equation}
evaluated with upper incomplete gamma functions.

\emph{Pair expansions.} A pair at distance $d=|\mathbf P-\mathbf b|>\rho$
can be represented on the cluster by the Taylor polynomial of order $K=6$
of its exact potential about $\mathbf b$, whose coefficients are
$(-1)^{|\beta|}R_{\alpha+\beta}(p,\mathbf P-\mathbf b)/\beta!$. With $W_L$
the sum of $|e_k|$ over the terms of order $L$, the error on the cluster is
at most
\begin{equation}
 \sum_LW_L\Big[\sum_{n>K}\frac{(n+L)!}{n!}\frac{\rho^n}{d^{n+L+1}}
 +G_L(d-\rho)+\sum_{n\le K}\frac{(\sqrt3\rho)^n}{n!}G_{L+n}(d)\Big].
\end{equation}

\emph{Source clusters.} Pairs are grouped by the 2-bohr cell of their
centre and by exponent. For a group with centre $\mathbf c$ and radius $a$,
write $\mathbf P-\mathbf C=\mathbf X_0+\mathbf z$ with
$\mathbf X_0=\mathbf c-\mathbf b$ and
$\mathbf z=(\mathbf P-\mathbf c)-(\mathbf C-\mathbf b)$, $|\mathbf z|\le
a+\rho$. Expanding the point-multipole terms in $\mathbf z$ about
$\mathbf X_0$ to total order $N=10$ and splitting $\mathbf z^\gamma$
binomially gives a multipole-to-local translation of moments computed once
per group; its error is bounded by the same Taylor remainder with radius
$a+\rho$ and distance $|\mathbf X_0|$. The erfc terms are omitted and
bounded by $G_L$ for the smallest exponent of the group at distance
$|\mathbf X_0|-a-\rho$.

\emph{Error budget.} Half of the tolerance $\varepsilon$ is shared equally
by the groups (a quarter for truncation, a quarter for erfc terms); the
other half by the individually expanded pairs. Their bounds are tabulated
at levels $10^{-j/2}\varepsilon/2$, and in each cluster the pairs with the
smallest bounds are expanded while the sum fits in the budget; the others
are evaluated exactly. The error at every point is therefore at most
$\varepsilon$ (default $10^{-8}$ hartree/e; $\varepsilon=0$ is exact).

\section{Electrostatic potential on a molecular surface}\label{sec:surface}
The molecular surface is the isosurface $\rho(\mathbf r)=\rho_0$ of the
total density (default $\rho_0=10^{-3}$\,e/bohr$^3$), the region
$\rho\ge\rho_0$ inside.

\subsection{Isosurface}
The density is sampled on an axis-aligned grid of spacing $h$ (default
$0.25$\,bohr) whose boundary lies outside the isosurface. Each cell, with
corners $\mathbf g+\mathbf b$, $\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^3$ in grid units, is
split into the six tetrahedra
$\{\mathbf 0,\mathbf e_a,\mathbf e_a+\mathbf e_b,\mathbf 1\}$ for the
permutations $(a,b,c)$ of the axes (the Kuhn triangulation). Their edges
join $\mathbf u$ to $\mathbf u+\mathbf m$ with
$\mathbf m\in\{0,1\}^3\setminus\{\mathbf 0\}$, the same in every cell, so
the tetrahedra tile space conformingly. An edge whose ends lie on either
side of $\rho_0$ carries one vertex, shared by all tetrahedra around it;
a tetrahedron with one corner on one side contributes a triangle, one with
two corners on each side a quadrilateral, split along its shorter
diagonal. Each polygon is oriented by its edge midpoints so that its normal
points from the inside corners to the outside ones; the orientation is
therefore combinatorial and consistent between neighbours, and the mesh is
a closed oriented surface. Its enclosed volume follows from the divergence
theorem, $V=\frac16\sum_T(\mathbf x_0-\mathbf c)\cdot
[(\mathbf x_1-\mathbf c)\times(\mathbf x_2-\mathbf c)]$ for any point
$\mathbf c$.

The vertex on the edge from an inside point $\mathbf a$ to an outside point
$\mathbf b$ is the root of
$f(t)=\rho(\mathbf a+t(\mathbf b-\mathbf a))-\rho_0$ in $[0,1]$, started at
the linear interpolation of the samples. Newton steps
$t\leftarrow t-f(t)/f'(t)$, with $f'(t)=\nabla\rho\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)$
from the analytic gradient, are kept inside the bracket $[t_-,t_+]$ with
$f(t_-)\ge0>f(t_+)$, and replaced by bisection when they leave it, until
the step or the bracket is below $10^{-10}$\,bohr. The vertices therefore
lie on the isosurface of the analytic density (with the default AO
screening, which changes $\rho$ by about $10^{-13}$\,e/bohr$^3$, or
$10^{-10}$\,bohr in position at $\rho_0=10^{-3}$); the remaining
discretization error is that of the flat triangles, of order $h^2$.

\subsection{Statistics}
With $V$ the potential at the vertices, the statistics use its
piecewise-linear interpolation, integrated exactly. On a triangle of area
$A$ with corner values $w_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \int V\,dA=\frac A3\sum_iw_i,\qquad
 \int (V-s)^2\,dA=\frac A6\Big[\sum_i(w_i-s)^2+\sum_{i<j}(w_i-s)(w_j-s)\Big].
\end{equation}
A region $V\ge c$ or $V<c$ is clipped from each triangle along the line
$V=c$ of the interpolation (a triangle or a quadrilateral, split into
triangles). With $A_\pm$ the areas where $V\ge0$ and $V<0$,
$\overline V_\pm=A_\pm^{-1}\int_\pm V\,dA$ and
$\overline V=A^{-1}\int V\,dA$, the descriptors of Politzer and Murray are
\begin{gather}
 \sigma_\pm^2=\frac1{A_\pm}\int_\pm(V-\overline V_\pm)^2\,dA,\qquad
 \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^2=\sigma_+^2+\sigma_-^2,\qquad
 \nu=\frac{\sigma_+^2\sigma_-^2}{(\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^2)^2},\\
 \Pi=\frac1A\int|V-\overline V|\,dA,\qquad
 \mathrm{MPI}=\frac1A\int|V|\,dA,
\end{gather}
and the nonpolar area is that where $|V|\le10$\,kcal/mol. Surface extrema
are vertices whose value is below (above) that of their neighbours and
of the neighbours of those, as in Multiwfn. The potential at the vertices
uses the far-field approximations of the previous section, with clusters of
the vertices on the edges of blocks of $8\times8\times8$ cells.

Multiwfn's quantitative surface analysis (Lu and Chen, J.\ Mol.\ Graph.\
Model.\ 38, 314 (2012)) also triangulates by marching tetrahedra, but
places vertices by three bisections and linear interpolation, merges
vertices closer than $h/2$, and assigns each triangle the mean of its
vertex values; both discretizations converge to the same limit, and at
$h=0.05$\,bohr they agree to about $3\times10^{-5}$ relative on water.

\section{Validation and benchmark methodology}
AO, MO, and density spatial derivatives are checked against centered finite
differences of independent value/gradient evaluations. Finite differences
are used only for testing, never to compute production derivatives.
Density-matrix results are also checked against independent occupied-MO
sums for density, gradient, Hessian, and $\tau$, and blocked results are
compared with the pointwise reference. The regression suite includes Gaussian
09 reference Cube comparisons for density and signed orbital amplitudes, and
Multiwfn reference Cube files for ELF, the reduced gradient,
$\operatorname{sign}(\lambda_2)\rho$ and the electrostatic potential. The
electrostatic potential is also checked against the erf closed form for
s-function densities, against the multipole expansion of the analytic AO
moments far from the molecule, and against the Poisson equation
$\nabla^2V_{\mathrm{el}}=4\pi\rho$ by finite differences. The surface
construction and statistics are checked on a sphere and an octahedron with
closed-form answers, and the surface statistics of three molecules against
Multiwfn.
These tests establish agreement on the tested FCHK cases, not universal
support for every Gaussian basis or method.

The optional \texttt{kumo-spatial-benchmark} executable times AO values,
AO derivatives, representative dense contractions, density-only evaluation,
and pointwise/blocked density derivatives. The timer uses one warm-up and
the minimum of repeated wall-clock times. Representative contraction timings
are not additive components of end-to-end timings; the full methods have
different setup and allocation costs. Benchmark times are performance
measurements, never pass/fail correctness thresholds.

\section{Limits and intended use}
KUMO evaluates the real electronic fields defined by the supplied FCHK.
It does not perform SCF, orbital optimization, response theory, or nuclear
coordinate differentiation. The blocked derivative evaluator uses dense
matrices on the AOs that survive screening in fixed-size blocks, without
streaming output of derivative fields. Its mathematical derivatives are analytic
with respect to the supplied finite Gaussian representation; grid sampling
and floating-point arithmetic introduce their usual numerical limitations.
