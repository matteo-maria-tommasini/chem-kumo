\section{Scope, units and notation}

chem-kumo is a C++ library (with the command-line program \texttt{kumo})
that evaluates the electron density of a molecule, its spatial derivatives,
its molecular orbitals and a set of fields derived from them, starting from
a Gaussian formatted checkpoint (FCHK) file. The FCHK file supplies the
geometry, the Gaussian basis set, the self-consistent-field (SCF) density
matrices, the molecular-orbital (MO) coefficients and the orbital energies.
chem-kumo does not solve any electronic-structure equation: it neither
determines nor relaxes the electronic state, and it computes no derivative
with respect to the nuclear positions. Every derivative in this document is
taken with respect to the position $\mathbf r=(x,y,z)$ of an electron, with
the molecule held fixed.

\paragraph{Units.} Atomic units are used throughout: lengths in bohr
($a_0$), energies in hartree ($E_h$), charges in units of the elementary
charge $e$. Atomic and molecular orbitals have units of $a_0^{-3/2}$, the
density $e\,a_0^{-3}$, its gradient $e\,a_0^{-4}$, its Hessian and
Laplacian $e\,a_0^{-5}$, kinetic and energy densities $E_h\,a_0^{-3}$, and
the electrostatic potential $E_h/e$.

more than one section. Each symbol keeps one meaning in the whole document.

\section{Atomic orbitals and their derivatives}\label{sec:aos}

\subsection{Gaussian basis functions}
A Cartesian Gaussian primitive with exponent $\alpha$ centred at $\mathbf A$
is the function
\begin{equation}
 g_{\ell mn}(\mathbf r;\alpha)=N_{\ell mn}(\alpha)\,
 u^\ell v^m w^n\,e^{-\alpha(u^2+v^2+w^2)},\qquad
 (u,v,w)=\mathbf r-\mathbf A,
\end{equation}
where $N_{\ell mn}(\alpha)$ is its normalization constant and
$\ell+m+n$ is its angular momentum. An atomic orbital (a contracted
basis function) is a fixed linear combination of primitives on the same
centre, with contraction coefficients $d_{\mu p}$ read from the FCHK file:
\begin{equation}
 \chi_\mu(\mathbf r)=\sum_p d_{\mu p}\,g_{\mu p}(\mathbf r).
\end{equation}
Shells of pure (real spherical-harmonic) functions are obtained from the
Cartesian functions of the same shell by a fixed linear transformation,
which also puts them in the AO order of the FCHK file. Combined SP shells
use separate contraction coefficients for their S and P functions. The AO
order and normalization must be those of the MO coefficients and density
matrices of the same file, since these are expressed in that basis.

\subsection{Analytic derivatives up to second order}
Every primitive is a product of one factor per Cartesian direction, for
example $u^\ell e^{-\alpha u^2}$ along $x$. Its derivatives along $x$ are
again polynomials times the same exponential:
\begin{align}
 \frac{d}{du}\big(u^\ell e^{-\alpha u^2}\big)&=f'_\ell(u)\,e^{-\alpha u^2},&
 f'_\ell(u)&=\ell u^{\ell-1}-2\alpha u^{\ell+1},\\
 \frac{d^2}{du^2}\big(u^\ell e^{-\alpha u^2}\big)&=f''_\ell(u)\,e^{-\alpha u^2},&
 f''_\ell(u)&=\ell(\ell-1)u^{\ell-2}-2\alpha(2\ell+1)u^\ell+4\alpha^2u^{\ell+2}.
\end{align}
Terms with a zero prefactor (for example $\ell u^{\ell-1}$ with $\ell=0$)
are left out rather than evaluated, so that no negative power of $u$ is
formed at the centre. With
$G=N_{\ell mn}e^{-\alpha(u^2+v^2+w^2)}$, the first and second derivatives
of a primitive are
\begin{align}
 \partial_x g_{\ell mn}&=G\,f'_\ell(u)\,v^m w^n,\\
 \partial_{xx}g_{\ell mn}&=G\,f''_\ell(u)\,v^m w^n,\\
 \partial_{xy}g_{\ell mn}&=G\,f'_\ell(u)\,f'_m(v)\,w^n,
\end{align}
and the other components follow by exchanging the roles of $x,y,z$.
Differentiation commutes with the contraction and with the
Cartesian-to-pure transformation, so the derivatives of the AOs are
obtained by applying both to the derivatives of the primitives. For each AO
the evaluator returns the value, the three first derivatives and the six
distinct second derivatives, stored in the order $xx,yy,zz,xy,xz,yz$.

\subsection{Screening of negligible AOs}\label{sec:screening}
Gaussian functions decay quickly, so at any point most AOs of a large
molecule are negligible. chem-kumo uses two independent tests.

\emph{Shell cutoff (per point).} Each shell has a radius $r_{\mathrm{cut}}$
beyond which a rigorous bound on all its AOs and on their first and second
derivatives is below $10^{-14}$; at points farther than $r_{\mathrm{cut}}$
from the centre the AOs of the shell are set to exact zeros. For
$r=|\mathbf r-\mathbf A|\ge1$, every factor of the value and derivative
formulas of a primitive with exponent $a_p$ and Cartesian degree $\ell$ is
bounded by a multiple of $r^{\ell+2}e^{-a_pr^2}$, which gives
\begin{equation}
 |\chi|,\ |\partial_a\chi|,\ |\partial_{ab}\chi|\le
 T\sum_p W_p C_p\, r^{\ell+2}e^{-a_pr^2},\qquad
 C_p=1+\ell+\ell^2+2a_p(2\ell+2)+4a_p^2.
\end{equation}
Here $W_p$ is the largest contraction weight of primitive $p$ (coefficient
times normalization) and $T$ is the largest sum of absolute values in a row
of the Cartesian-to-pure transformation ($T=1$ for Cartesian shells). Each
term decreases for $r>\sqrt{(\ell+2)/(2a_p)}$, and $r_{\mathrm{cut}}$ is
found by bisection.

\emph{AO screening (per block of points).} Fields are evaluated on blocks
of nearby points (Section~\ref{sec:contraction}). In a block, an AO is
\emph{screened} when its value, and its first and second derivatives if
they are needed, are at most $\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}$ in absolute value at
every point of the block (default $\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}=10^{-12}$).
Screened AOs are removed from the block: their rows are deleted from the
AO values, and their rows and columns from the density matrix (or their
rows from the MO coefficients). The products described below then involve
only the AOs that matter in that block. The resulting change of the density
is at the level of rounding in the tested molecules, and a threshold of
zero gives the exact contraction.

\section{Molecular orbitals and density matrices}\label{sec:mos}

\subsection{Molecular orbitals}
A molecular orbital of spin channel $\sigma$ and its derivatives are linear
combinations of the AOs and of their derivatives:
\begin{equation}
 \psi_i^\sigma(\mathbf r)=\sum_\mu C_{\mu i}^\sigma\chi_\mu(\mathbf r),\qquad
 \partial_a\psi_i^\sigma=\sum_\mu C_{\mu i}^\sigma\,\partial_a\chi_\mu,\qquad
 \partial_{ab}\psi_i^\sigma=\sum_\mu C_{\mu i}^\sigma\,\partial_{ab}\chi_\mu.
\end{equation}
These are the signed orbital amplitudes (not their squares); their overall
sign is that of the FCHK coefficients, with no phase convention applied.
In a restricted calculation the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ channels share one
set of spatial orbitals; in an unrestricted one each channel has its own.
MO indices are 1-based in the public interfaces.

\subsection{Density matrices and spin components}
The density of channel $\sigma$ is a quadratic form in the AO values,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_\sigma(\mathbf r)=\boldsymbol\chi(\mathbf r)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P^\sigma
 \boldsymbol\chi(\mathbf r)=\sum_{\mu\nu}P^\sigma_{\mu\nu}\chi_\mu\chi_\nu,
\end{equation}
with a real symmetric density matrix $\mathbf P^\sigma$. For an SCF wave
function with integer occupations,
$\mathbf P^\sigma=\sum_i n_i^\sigma\,\mathbf C^\sigma_i(\mathbf C^\sigma_i)^{\mathsf T}$,
where $\mathbf C^\sigma_i$ is the coefficient column of MO $i$, so that
$\rho_\sigma=\sum_in_i^\sigma(\psi_i^\sigma)^2$.

The FCHK file stores the \emph{total} matrix
$\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}=\mathbf P^\alpha+\mathbf P^\beta$ and, for open
shells, the \emph{spin-difference} matrix
$\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}=\mathbf P^\alpha-\mathbf P^\beta$ (for closed shells
$\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}=0$). These two matrices are the reference data of
chem-kumo. The spin matrices are obtained from them as
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf P^\alpha=\tfrac12(\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}+\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}),\qquad
 \mathbf P^\beta=\tfrac12(\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}-\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}),
\end{equation}
never from the MO occupations. Four density components can be requested:
the total density $\rho=\rho_\alpha+\rho_\beta$, the spin densities
$\rho_\alpha$ and $\rho_\beta$, and the spin-difference density
$\rho_\alpha-\rho_\beta$, which can be negative.

\section{The density and its spatial derivatives}\label{sec:derivatives}

Let $\mathbf P$ be any of the four matrices above. Write
$\boldsymbol\chi_a=\partial_a\boldsymbol\chi$ and
$\boldsymbol\chi_{ab}=\partial_{ab}\boldsymbol\chi$ for the vectors of AO
derivatives. Differentiating the quadratic form and using the symmetry of
$\mathbf P$ gives
\begin{align}
 \rho&=\boldsymbol\chi^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\boldsymbol\chi,\\
 \partial_a\rho&=2\,\boldsymbol\chi_a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\boldsymbol\chi,\\
 \partial_{ab}\rho&=2\,\boldsymbol\chi_{ab}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\boldsymbol\chi
       +2\,\boldsymbol\chi_a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\boldsymbol\chi_b,\\
 \nabla^2\rho&=\partial_{xx}\rho+\partial_{yy}\rho+\partial_{zz}\rho
 =\operatorname{tr}\mathbf H .
\end{align}
All nine Hessian components, off-diagonal ones included, are computed
analytically. The positive kinetic-energy density of a spin channel is
\begin{equation}
 \tau_\sigma=\frac12\sum_{a}\boldsymbol\chi_a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P^\sigma\boldsymbol\chi_a
 =\frac12\sum_i n_i^\sigma\,|\nabla\psi_i^\sigma|^2\ \ge0,
\end{equation}
and $\tau=\tau_\alpha+\tau_\beta$. The same formula with
$\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}$ gives $\tau_\alpha-\tau_\beta$, which can be
negative. Since the Laplacian of the density can be written as
\begin{equation}
 \nabla^2\rho=2\,(\nabla^2\boldsymbol\chi)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\boldsymbol\chi+4\tau,
\end{equation}
the tests use this identity as an independent consistency check.

\section{Evaluating the density on many points}\label{sec:contraction}

Fields are evaluated on blocks of $B$ points at a time (by default
$B=128$; at most 65\,536). For a block, the values of all AOs form an
$N_{\mathrm{AO}}\times B$ matrix $\Phi$ (one column per point), and their
first derivatives the matrices $\Phi_x,\Phi_y,\Phi_z$; the second
derivatives form six more matrices $\Phi_{ab}$. chem-kumo offers two
equivalent ways of contracting them into densities: with the density matrix
(AO-P) or with the occupied MOs (AO-MO).

\subsection{Density-matrix contraction (AO-P)}
The value and first-derivative matrices are placed side by side,
$\mathbf A=[\Phi\ \Phi_x\ \Phi_y\ \Phi_z]$ of size
$N_{\mathrm{AO}}\times4B$, and the density matrix is applied to all of them
in a single matrix product,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf W=\mathbf P\mathbf A=[\mathbf W_0\ \mathbf W_x\ \mathbf W_y\ \mathbf W_z].
\end{equation}
With $\mathbf v_j$ denoting column $j$ of a matrix $\mathbf v$, the fields
at point $j$ of the block are then dot products of columns:
\begin{align}
 \rho_j&=\Phi_j^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf W_0)_j,\qquad
 (\partial_a\rho)_j=2(\Phi_a)_j^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf W_0)_j,\\
 (\partial_{ab}\rho)_j&=2(\Phi_{ab})_j^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf W_0)_j
                  +2(\Phi_a)_j^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf W_b)_j,\qquad
 \tau_j=\tfrac12\sum_a(\Phi_a)_j^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf W_a)_j.
\end{align}
The second-derivative matrices enter only these dot products and are never
multiplied by $\mathbf P$. The product costs about $4N_{\mathrm{AO}}^2B$
multiply--add operations per block (fewer after screening), which dominates
the cost; the dot products cost $O(N_{\mathrm{AO}}B)$.

When several spin components are requested, the total and spin-difference
matrices are each applied once, and the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ fields are
obtained from the results as $\frac12(\text{total}\pm\text{difference})$:
every quantity above is linear in $\mathbf P$. For the density alone on a
grid the roles are exchanged: if $\rho_\alpha$ or $\rho_\beta$ is
requested, $\mathbf P^\alpha$ and $\mathbf P^\beta$ are applied and the
total and difference densities are their sum and difference, so that no
request needs more than two products.

\subsection{Occupied-orbital contraction (AO-MO)}
When the density comes from occupied orbitals with occupation one per spin
orbital, the same fields follow from the occupied orbital amplitudes. Let
$\mathbf C^\sigma_{\mathrm{occ}}$ be the $N_{\mathrm{AO}}\times N_\sigma$
matrix of the coefficients of the $N_\sigma$ occupied orbitals of channel
$\sigma$. One product per AO matrix gives the orbital amplitudes and their
derivatives on the block,
\begin{equation}
 \Psi=(\mathbf C^\sigma_{\mathrm{occ}})^{\mathsf T}\Phi,\qquad
 \Psi_a=(\mathbf C^\sigma_{\mathrm{occ}})^{\mathsf T}\Phi_a,\qquad
 \Psi_{ab}=(\mathbf C^\sigma_{\mathrm{occ}})^{\mathsf T}\Phi_{ab},
\end{equation}
and, at point $j$,
\begin{align}
 \rho_{\sigma,j}&=|\Psi_j|^2,\qquad
 (\partial_a\rho_\sigma)_j=2\,\Psi_j^{\mathsf T}(\Psi_a)_j,\\
 (\partial_{ab}\rho_\sigma)_j&=2\,\Psi_j^{\mathsf T}(\Psi_{ab})_j
   +2\,(\Psi_a)_j^{\mathsf T}(\Psi_b)_j,\qquad
 \tau_{\sigma,j}=\tfrac12\sum_a|(\Psi_a)_j|^2 .
\end{align}
The total and spin-difference fields are the sum and difference of the two
channels. For a restricted closed shell the $\beta$ orbitals are the
$\alpha$ ones, and the $\beta$ channel is copied rather than recomputed.
Each product costs $N_{\mathrm{AO}}N_\sigma B$ operations, usually much
less than the AO-P product because $N_\sigma\ll N_{\mathrm{AO}}$.

\paragraph{Eligibility check.} The two contractions agree only if the
occupied orbitals reproduce the density matrices of the file, which is not
the case, for example, for post-SCF (correlated) densities. When the file is
loaded, chem-kumo rebuilds
$\tilde{\mathbf P}^\sigma=\mathbf C^\sigma_{\mathrm{occ}}(\mathbf C^\sigma_{\mathrm{occ}})^{\mathsf T}$,
$\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm t}=\tilde{\mathbf P}^\alpha+\tilde{\mathbf P}^\beta$ and
$\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm s}=\tilde{\mathbf P}^\alpha-\tilde{\mathbf P}^\beta$
and compares them with the stored matrices in the Frobenius norm. With the
scales
$S_{\mathrm t}=\max(\|\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}\|,\|\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm t}\|)$ and
$S_{\mathrm s}=\max(\|\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}\|,\|\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm s}\|,S_{\mathrm t})$,
the orbitals are accepted (AO-MO \emph{eligible}) when
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}-\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm t}\|\le
 \epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}S_{\mathrm t}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \|\mathbf P^{\mathrm s}-\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm s}\|\le
 \epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}S_{\mathrm s},
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}=10^{-8}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}=10^{-10}$.
The spin difference is measured on the scale of the total matrix because
$\tilde{\mathbf P}^{\mathrm s}$ is a difference of two matrices of that
size: the rounding of the stored coefficients (about nine significant
digits) gives it an error proportional to $\|\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}\|$, which
would fail a check relative to a small spin matrix (for example in a weakly
spin-polarized broken-symmetry state).

\paragraph{Choice of the contraction.} By default (\emph{automatic}
backend) the AO-MO contraction is used when the file is eligible and the
request involves a density; otherwise AO-P is used. AO-P can always be
requested explicitly; an explicit AO-MO request for an ineligible file is
an error, never silently replaced by AO-P.

\section{Grids, lines, planes and points}\label{sec:grids}

\subsection{Grids}
A grid is defined by an origin $\mathbf o$, three step vectors
$\mathbf s_1,\mathbf s_2,\mathbf s_3$ (not necessarily orthogonal) and the
point counts $n_1,n_2,n_3$. Its points are
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf r_{ijk}=\mathbf o+i\,\mathbf s_1+j\,\mathbf s_2+k\,\mathbf s_3,
 \qquad 0\le i<n_1,\ 0\le j<n_2,\ 0\le k<n_3,
\end{equation}
stored with $k$ varying fastest, as in Gaussian Cube files (linear index
$(i\,n_2+j)\,n_3+k$). A count of 1 along an axis is allowed, so lines and
planes are grids too.

\paragraph{Grid around the molecule.} Given a margin $m$ and a spacing $h$,
the grid covers the smallest axis-aligned box containing the nuclei,
enlarged by $m$ on every side. Along axis $a$, with $\ell_a$ the extent of
the nuclei,
\begin{equation}
 n_a=\max\!\Big(2,\ \Big\lceil\frac{\ell_a+2m}{h}\Big\rceil+1\Big),\qquad
 \mathbf s_a=h\,\mathbf e_a ,
\end{equation}
and the origin is the lowest corner of the enlarged box. The spacing is
exactly $h$, so the grid may extend slightly beyond the margin on the upper
side.

\paragraph{Lines and planes.} A line from $\mathbf p$ to $\mathbf q$ (and a
plane through three points) uses the fewest evenly spaced points whose
spacing does not exceed $h$, with both ends included, so that the profile
starts and ends exactly at the requested points. For a segment of length
$L$ (including the margins) the number of points is
\begin{equation}
 n=\max\!\Big(2,\ \Big\lceil\frac{L}{h}(1-10^{-12})\Big\rceil+1\Big),
 \qquad\text{spacing } L/(n-1)\le h .
\end{equation}
The factor $1-10^{-12}$ makes a length that is a multiple of $h$ up to
rounding (for example $29.000000000000004\,h$) keep that multiple.

For a line, $L=|\mathbf q-\mathbf p|+2m$ and the line starts at
$\mathbf p-m\,\mathbf d$, with $\mathbf d$ the unit vector from $\mathbf p$
to $\mathbf q$. The two other axes are unit vectors orthogonal to the line,
each with one point.

For a plane through $\mathbf a$, $\mathbf b$, $\mathbf c$, the in-plane
axes are $\mathbf u=(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)/|\mathbf b-\mathbf a|$ and the unit
vector $\mathbf v$ along the component of $\mathbf c-\mathbf a$ orthogonal
to $\mathbf u$. In these coordinates the three points are $(0,0)$,
$(|\mathbf b-\mathbf a|,0)$ and $(c_u,c_v)$ with $c_v>0$; the plane covers
the rectangle $[\min(0,c_u)-m,\ \max(|\mathbf b-\mathbf a|,c_u)+m]\times
[-m,\ c_v+m]$, with the point counts above along each side and one point
along the normal. The three points must not be collinear:
$|(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)|$ must exceed
$10^{-8}\,|\mathbf b-\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf c-\mathbf a|$.

\subsection{Arbitrary points}
Fields can also be evaluated at a list of points in any order. The points
are processed in consecutive blocks of the list, with the same formulas as
on grids; only the electrostatic potential groups them differently
(Section~\ref{sec:esp-clusters}). The values agree with those of a grid
evaluation at the same points to rounding.

\section{Fields derived from the density}\label{sec:descriptors}

The fields of this section are computed at each point from the density,
its gradient, its Hessian and $\tau$ (Section~\ref{sec:derivatives}).
Several of them divide by the density; Table~\ref{tab:conventions} at the
end of the section gives the value used where the density vanishes.

\subsection{Electron localization function (ELF)}
The ELF of Becke and Edgecombe compares the Pauli kinetic-energy density,
the part of $\tau$ due to the Pauli principle, with its value in a uniform
electron gas of the same density. For a spin channel,
\begin{equation}
 D_\sigma=\tau_\sigma-\frac{|\nabla\rho_\sigma|^2}{8\rho_\sigma},\qquad
 D^0_\sigma=\frac{3}{10}(6\pi^2)^{2/3}\rho_\sigma^{5/3},\qquad
 \mathrm{ELF}=\frac{1}{1+(D/D^0)^2}.
\end{equation}
For the ELF of a spin channel, $D$ and $D^0$ are those of the channel; for
the total ELF they are the sums over $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (the
spin-polarized form). For a closed shell this reduces to the familiar
$D=\tau-|\nabla\rho|^2/(8\rho)$ and $D^0=\frac{3}{10}(3\pi^2)^{2/3}\rho^{5/3}$.
Channels without density are left out of the sums. The Pauli term equals
the orbital form
\begin{equation}
 D_\sigma=\frac{1}{2\rho_\sigma}\sum_{i<j}
 n_i^\sigma n_j^\sigma\,|\psi_i^\sigma\nabla\psi_j^\sigma-\psi_j^\sigma\nabla\psi_i^\sigma|^2,
\end{equation}
which is free of cancellation and is used in the tests as an independent
check.

\subsection{Localized orbital locator (LOL)}
The LOL of Schmider and Becke uses the same ingredients:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{LOL}=\frac{t}{1+t},\qquad t=\frac{D^0}{\tau},
\end{equation}
with $\tau$ and $D^0$ summed over the two channels for the total LOL.

\subsection{Non-covalent interactions}
The reduced density gradient of the total density,
\begin{equation}
 s=\frac{|\nabla\rho|}{2(3\pi^2)^{1/3}\rho^{4/3}},
\end{equation}
is small where the density varies slowly, for example between weakly
interacting fragments. The sign of the middle eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ of the
Hessian ($\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3$) distinguishes attractive
($\lambda_2<0$) from repulsive ($\lambda_2>0$) interactions; the field
$\operatorname{sign}(\lambda_2)\rho$ counts $\lambda_2=0$ as positive.

The interaction region indicator of Lu and Chen is
$\mathrm{IRI}=|\nabla\rho|/\rho^{1.1}$. The density overlap regions
indicator of de Silva and Corminboeuf is
$\mathrm{DORI}=\theta/(1+\theta)$, with
\begin{equation}
 \theta=\frac{|\nabla f|^2}{f^3},\qquad f=\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho^2}.
\end{equation}
With $\mathbf q=\nabla\rho/\rho$ this becomes
\begin{equation}
 \theta=\frac{4\,|(\mathbf H/\rho)\,\mathbf q-|\mathbf q|^2\mathbf q|^2}{|\mathbf q|^6},
\end{equation}
which is the form computed: it involves only ratios of derivatives to the
density, so it does not overflow or underflow where the density is very
small or very large.

\subsection{Energy densities and information densities}
Three energy densities follow from $\tau$ and the Laplacian. The Hamiltonian
kinetic-energy density $\mathcal K$, the potential energy density
$\mathcal V$ (from the local virial theorem) and the energy density
$\mathcal H$ are
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K=-\frac12\sum_in_i\psi_i\nabla^2\psi_i=\tau-\frac{\nabla^2\rho}{4},\qquad
 \mathcal V=\frac{\nabla^2\rho}{4}-2\tau,\qquad
 \mathcal H=\tau+\mathcal V=-\mathcal K .
\end{equation}
The spin polarization is $(\rho_\alpha-\rho_\beta)/\rho$. The Shannon
entropy density is $-\rho\ln\rho$ and the Fisher information density is
$|\nabla\rho|^2/\rho$.

\section{Fields of the orbital energies}\label{sec:orbital-energies}

These fields weight the orbital densities $|\psi_i(\mathbf r)|^2$ by the
orbital energies $\varepsilon_i$. Sums over $i$ run over the MOs of both
spin channels (for a restricted calculation, over the spatial orbitals
with the occupations given below). The definitions are those of Multiwfn.

\paragraph{Average local ionization energy.} The average local ionization
energy of Sj\"oberg, Murray, Brinck and Politzer is
\begin{equation}
 \bar I(\mathbf r)=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathrm{occ}}n_i|\varepsilon_i|\,|\psi_i(\mathbf r)|^2}
 {\sum_{i\in\mathrm{occ}}n_i|\psi_i(\mathbf r)|^2},
\end{equation}
over the occupied orbitals, with $n_i=2$ for the doubly occupied orbitals of
a restricted calculation and $n_i=1$ otherwise. It is a weighted average of
the $|\varepsilon_i|$, so it lies between the smallest and the largest of
them, and low values mark the electrons that are easiest to remove. The
denominator is the total density only if the occupied orbitals reproduce
the density matrices, so the file must be AO-MO eligible
(Section~\ref{sec:contraction}); other files are rejected.

\paragraph{Fields of the virtual orbitals.} The local electron affinity,
the local electron attachment energy, and the local electronegativity and
hardness are
\begin{gather}
 \mathrm{EA}_L(\mathbf r)=-\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathrm{vir}}\varepsilon_i|\psi_i(\mathbf r)|^2}
 {\sum_{i\in\mathrm{vir}}|\psi_i(\mathbf r)|^2},\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{att}}(\mathbf r)=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathrm{vir},\,\varepsilon_i<0}
 w\,\varepsilon_i|\psi_i(\mathbf r)|^2}{\rho(\mathbf r)},\\
 \chi_L=\tfrac12(\bar I+\mathrm{EA}_L),\qquad \eta_L=\tfrac12(\bar I-\mathrm{EA}_L),
\end{gather}
where the virtual orbitals are the MOs of the file above the occupied ones
in each channel. The virtual orbitals are empty, so the weight $w$ is not
an occupation: it counts the spin orbitals of a spatial orbital, $w=2$ for
a restricted and $w=1$ for an unrestricted calculation (such a weight would
cancel in $\mathrm{EA}_L$).
$\chi_L$ and $\eta_L$ are local analogues of Mulliken's electronegativity
$(I+A)/2$ and of the chemical hardness $(I-A)/2$.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} The four orbital sums (occupied density and
energy, virtual density and energy) and the attachment sum are accumulated
on blocks of points from the orbital amplitudes
$(\mathbf C_{\mathrm{occ}})^{\mathsf T}\Phi$ and
$(\mathbf C_{\mathrm{vir}})^{\mathsf T}\Phi$, without AO screening. Each
field is set to 0 where its denominator vanishes.

\paragraph{Restricted open shells.} The occupations above include the case
of a restricted open-shell calculation (singly occupied orbitals with
$n_i=1$), but chem-kumo cannot load such files yet: Gaussian writes no
$\beta$ MO coefficients for them, and the loader rejects open-shell files
without $\beta$ coefficients.

\section{Molecular electrostatic potential}\label{sec:esp}

The electrostatic potential at $\mathbf r$ is the potential of the nuclei
minus that of the electron density:
\begin{equation}
 V(\mathbf r)=\sum_A\frac{Z_A}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf R_A|}
 -\sum_{\mu\nu}P^{\mathrm t}_{\mu\nu}\int\frac{\chi_\mu(\mathbf r')\chi_\nu(\mathbf r')}
 {|\mathbf r'-\mathbf r|}\,d\mathbf r'.
\end{equation}
The nuclear charges $Z_A$ are those of the file (the effective charges when
effective core potentials are used). At a point within $10^{-8}$ bohr of a
nucleus, the singular term of that nucleus is left out, which gives the
potential at the nucleus due to the electrons and the other nuclei.

\subsection{Electron integrals by Hermite expansion}
Pure shells are handled by transforming $\mathbf P^{\mathrm t}$ to the
Cartesian functions. The product of two Cartesian primitives with exponents
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ centred at $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf B$ is a Gaussian
with exponent $p=\alpha+\beta$, centred at
$\mathbf Q=(\alpha\mathbf A+\beta\mathbf B)/p$, times the constant
$\kappa=\exp(-\alpha\beta|\mathbf A-\mathbf B|^2/p)$ and a polynomial. In
the McMurchie--Davidson scheme the polynomial is expanded in the Hermite
Gaussians
$\Lambda_{tuv}=\partial^t_{Q_x}\partial^u_{Q_y}\partial^v_{Q_z}e^{-p|\mathbf r-\mathbf Q|^2}$;
along $x$, the expansion coefficients of $x_A^ix_B^j$ (with
$x_A=x-A_x$, $x_B=x-B_x$) obey
\begin{equation}
 E^{i+1,j}_t=\frac{1}{2p}E^{ij}_{t-1}+(Q_x-A_x)E^{ij}_t+(t+1)E^{ij}_{t+1},
 \qquad E^{00}_0=1,
\end{equation}
and likewise for $j$ with $Q_x-B_x$. The Coulomb integral of a Hermite
Gaussian is
$\int\Lambda_{tuv}(\mathbf r')/|\mathbf r'-\mathbf r|\,d\mathbf r'
=\frac{2\pi}{p}R_{tuv}(p,\mathbf Q-\mathbf r)$, with the auxiliary
integrals
\begin{align}
 R^n_{000}&=(-2p)^nF_n\big(p|\mathbf Q-\mathbf r|^2\big),\\
 R^n_{t+1,u,v}&=t\,R^{n+1}_{t-1,u,v}+(Q_x-r_x)\,R^{n+1}_{tuv}
\end{align}
(and likewise for $u$ and $v$), $R_{tuv}=R^0_{tuv}$, and $F_n$ the Boys
function below. The density matrix is contracted with the Hermite
coefficients once, when the potential is set up: each primitive pair then
has coefficients $c_{tuv}$ (including $\kappa$, the contraction and
normalization factors and $2\pi/p$) such that its contribution to the
electronic potential is $-\sum_{tuv}c_{tuv}R_{tuv}(p,\mathbf Q-\mathbf r)$.

\subsection{Boys function}
The Boys function $F_m(T)=\int_0^1t^{2m}e^{-Tt^2}\,dt$ is needed for
$m\le32$. For $T<40$ the highest order needed is computed by a Taylor
polynomial of degree six (seven terms) about the nearest point of a table with
spacing 0.05, using $dF_m/dT=-F_{m+1}$; the table is built from the series
of positive terms
$F_M(T)=e^{-T}\sum_{k\ge0}(2T)^k/[(2M+1)(2M+3)\cdots(2M+2k+1)]$. Lower
orders follow from the downward recursion
$F_{m-1}=(2TF_m+e^{-T})/(2m-1)$, which is numerically stable. For
$T\ge40$, $F_0=\frac12\sqrt{\pi/T}$ to double precision (the error function
differs from 1 by less than $10^{-18}$), and the upward recursion
$F_{m+1}=[(2m+1)F_m-e^{-T}]/(2T)$ is stable. The result agrees with
numerical quadrature of the definition to $2.3\times10^{-15}$ relative.

\subsection{Far-field approximations}\label{sec:far-field}
Computing every primitive pair exactly at every point is expensive for
large grids, so pairs far from a group of nearby evaluation points are
represented by expansions about the centre of the group. The evaluation
points are grouped into \emph{target clusters} (Section~\ref{sec:esp-clusters}),
each with a centre $\mathbf c$ and a radius $r_{\mathrm t}$ that contains
all its points. The expansions are chosen so that the error at every point
is at most a tolerance $\varepsilon$ (default $10^{-8}$\,$E_h/e$;
$\varepsilon=0$ gives the exact sum).

\paragraph{Splitting a pair.} With $\mathbf X=\mathbf Q-\mathbf r$ and
$X=|\mathbf X|$, the identity
$F_0(pX^2)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt p\,X}\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt pX)$ splits
the potential of a pair into a point-multipole part and a short-range part:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_kc_kR_{\alpha_k}(p,\mathbf X)
 =\sum_ke_k\,\partial^{\alpha_k}\frac1X
 -\sum_ke_k\,\partial^{\alpha_k}\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt pX)}{X},
 \qquad e_k=\frac{c_k}{2}\sqrt{\frac\pi p},
\end{equation}
where $k$ runs over the Hermite terms $\alpha_k=(t,u,v)$ of the pair. The
short-range part decays like $e^{-pX^2}$.

\paragraph{Two derivative bounds.} The errors below are bounded with two
results. (i) Every derivative of order $m$ of $1/|\mathbf x|$, viewed as a
symmetric multilinear form, has norm at most $m!/|\mathbf x|^{m+1}$: the
directional derivatives are Legendre polynomials, bounded by 1, and the
norm of a symmetric form on a Hilbert space equals the maximum of its
values on the diagonal (Banach's theorem). (ii) Every derivative of order
$m$ of
$\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt pX)/X=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\sqrt p}^\infty
e^{-s^2X^2}ds$ at $X\ge r$ is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 G_m(r)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\,c_{\mathrm C}^3\sqrt{2^mm!}
 \int_{\sqrt p}^\infty s^me^{-s^2r^2/2}\,ds,
\end{equation}
obtained by applying Cram\'er's inequality
$|H_n(x)|e^{-x^2/2}\le c_{\mathrm C}\sqrt{2^nn!}$ ($c_{\mathrm C}<1.0865$)
to each Cartesian factor of the derivative; $G_m$ is evaluated with upper
incomplete gamma functions.

\paragraph{Expansion of single pairs.} A pair whose centre is at distance
$d=|\mathbf Q-\mathbf c|>r_{\mathrm t}$ from the cluster centre can be
replaced, on the whole cluster, by the Taylor polynomial of order $K=6$ of
its exact potential about $\mathbf c$, whose coefficients are
$(-1)^{|\beta|}R_{\alpha+\beta}(p,\mathbf Q-\mathbf c)/\beta!$ for the
multi-indices $\beta$ with $|\beta|\le K$. Let $W_L$ be the sum of $|e_k|$
over the Hermite terms of order $L=t+u+v$. The error on the cluster is at
most
\begin{equation}
 \sum_LW_L\Big[\sum_{n>K}\frac{(n+L)!}{n!}\frac{r_{\mathrm t}^n}{d^{n+L+1}}
 +G_L(d-r_{\mathrm t})+\sum_{n\le K}\frac{(\sqrt3\,r_{\mathrm t})^n}{n!}G_{L+n}(d)\Big]:
\end{equation}
the first term is the Taylor remainder of the point multipoles, the second
the neglected short-range part at the points, and the third the
short-range part included in the Taylor coefficients.

\paragraph{Source clusters.} Pairs are also grouped into \emph{source
clusters}: pairs whose centres lie in the same cube of edge 2 bohr (aligned
with the axes) and whose exponents lie in the same interval
$[4^k,4^{k+1})$. For a source cluster with centre $\mathbf s$ and radius
$r_{\mathrm s}$, write
$\mathbf Q-\mathbf r=\mathbf X_0+\mathbf z$ with $\mathbf X_0=\mathbf s-\mathbf c$
and $\mathbf z=(\mathbf Q-\mathbf s)-(\mathbf r-\mathbf c)$, so that
$|\mathbf z|\le r_{\mathrm s}+r_{\mathrm t}$. Expanding the point-multipole
terms in $\mathbf z$ about $\mathbf X_0$ to total order $N=10$, and
splitting $\mathbf z^\gamma$ by the binomial theorem into powers of
$\mathbf Q-\mathbf s$ and $\mathbf r-\mathbf c$, gives a
multipole-to-local translation: the multipole moments of the source cluster
are computed once, and one translation per target cluster gives a local
polynomial about $\mathbf c$. Its error is bounded by the same Taylor
remainder with radius $r_{\mathrm s}+r_{\mathrm t}$ and distance
$|\mathbf X_0|$. The short-range terms are left out and bounded by $G_L$
for the smallest exponent of the cluster at distance
$|\mathbf X_0|-r_{\mathrm s}-r_{\mathrm t}$.

\paragraph{Error budget.} Half of the tolerance $\varepsilon$ is divided
equally among the source clusters (a quarter for the truncation of the
expansion, a quarter for the neglected short-range terms); the other half
is for the individually expanded pairs. Their bounds are tabulated, as
functions of the distance, at the levels $10^{-j/2}\varepsilon/2$
($j=0,\dots,32$). In each target cluster, the pairs with the smallest
bounds are expanded as long as the sum of their bounds fits in the budget;
the remaining pairs are computed exactly. The error at every point is
therefore at most $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Target clusters}\label{sec:esp-clusters}
The points are grouped into target clusters in three ways, depending on
where they come from; in each case one far-field plan, built for the
largest cluster radius, serves all clusters.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Grids:} tiles of $8\times8\times8$ grid points (smaller at the
 upper edges of the grid), centred at the centre of their index box; the
 radius is the largest distance from the centre of the largest tile to its
 corners.
\item \emph{Isosurface vertices} (Section~\ref{sec:surfaces}): each vertex
 lies on a grid edge, and the vertices are grouped by the block of
 $8\times8\times8$ grid cells that contains the lower end of their edge.
 Such a vertex lies in the index box $[8T,8T+8]$ along each axis (block
 $T$), so the centre is at index $8T+4$ and the radius is half the diagonal
 of a box of 8 steps.
\item \emph{Arbitrary points:} the points are grouped by the cube of edge
 1.6 bohr, aligned with the axes at multiples of the edge, that contains
 them; the centre is the centre of the cube and the radius half its
 diagonal, $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\times1.6$ bohr.
\end{itemize}
The radius is enlarged by a relative $10^{-10}$ plus $10^{-12}$ bohr to
cover the rounding of the point coordinates. Clusters depend only on the
points, and each value only on its point, its cluster centre and the plan,
so the results do not depend on the order in which the clusters are
processed or on the number of threads.

\section{Molecular surfaces}\label{sec:surfaces}

The molecular surface is the isosurface $\rho(\mathbf r)=\rho_0$ of the
total density (by default $\rho_0=10^{-3}\,e\,a_0^{-3}$), with the region
$\rho\ge\rho_0$ inside. Any one-component field can be evaluated at its
vertices and summarized by area-weighted statistics.

\subsection{Triangulation}
The density is sampled on a grid around the molecule with spacing $h$
(default $0.25$ bohr) whose outer faces lie entirely outside the
isosurface; if the default margin of 4 bohr is not enough, it is increased
in steps of 2 bohr up to 16 bohr. Each grid cell, with corners at the
integer offsets $\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^3$ from its lowest corner, is split
into six tetrahedra $\{\mathbf 0,\mathbf e_a,\mathbf e_a+\mathbf e_b,\mathbf 1\}$,
one for each ordering $(a,b,c)$ of the three axes (the Kuhn
triangulation). The edges of these tetrahedra join a grid point
$\mathbf u$ to $\mathbf u+\mathbf m$ with $\mathbf m\in\{0,1\}^3\setminus\{\mathbf 0\}$,
the same in every cell, so the tetrahedra of neighbouring cells share their
faces and edges.

An edge whose two ends lie on different sides of $\rho_0$ carries one
vertex of the surface, shared by all tetrahedra around that edge. A
tetrahedron with one corner on one side and three on the other contributes
a triangle; one with two corners on each side contributes a quadrilateral,
split into two triangles along its shorter diagonal. Each polygon is
oriented so that its normal points from the inside corners to the outside
ones, using the midpoints of its edges; the orientation depends only on
which corners are inside, so neighbouring polygons agree and the mesh is a
closed oriented surface. Its enclosed volume follows from the divergence
theorem,
$\Omega=\frac16\sum_{\text{triangles}}(\mathbf x_0-\mathbf p)\cdot
[(\mathbf x_1-\mathbf p)\times(\mathbf x_2-\mathbf p)]$ for any point
$\mathbf p$, with $\mathbf x_0,\mathbf x_1,\mathbf x_2$ the corners of a
triangle in counter-clockwise order seen from outside.

\subsection{Placing the vertices on the isosurface}
The vertex on the edge from an inside grid point $\mathbf a$ to an outside
grid point $\mathbf b$ is the root in $[0,1]$ of
$f(t)=\rho(\mathbf a+t(\mathbf b-\mathbf a))-\rho_0$. The search starts at
the linear interpolation of the two samples and uses Newton steps
$t\leftarrow t-f(t)/f'(t)$, with
$f'(t)=\nabla\rho\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)$ from the analytic gradient. A
bracket $[t_-,t_+]$ with $f(t_-)\ge0>f(t_+)$ is kept, and a Newton step
that leaves the bracket is replaced by bisection. The iteration stops when
the step or the bracket is shorter than $10^{-10}$ bohr. The vertices
therefore lie on the isosurface of the analytic density; the default AO
screening changes $\rho$ by about $10^{-13}\,e\,a_0^{-3}$, which moves the
isosurface $\rho_0=10^{-3}$ by about $10^{-10}$ bohr. The remaining error
is that of representing a curved surface by flat triangles, of order
$h^2$.

\subsection{Surface statistics}
Let $w$ be the values of a field at the vertices, interpolated linearly on
each triangle; all integrals below are exact for this interpolation. On a
triangle of area $A_T$ with corner values $w_1,w_2,w_3$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_T w\,dA=\frac{A_T}3\sum_iw_i,\qquad
 \int_T (w-c)^2\,dA=\frac{A_T}6\Big[\sum_i(w_i-c)^2+\sum_{i<j}(w_i-c)(w_j-c)\Big].
\end{equation}
The part of a triangle where $w\ge c$ (or $w<c$) is cut along the line
$w=c$ of the interpolation (a triangle or a quadrilateral, split into
triangles). With $A$ the total area, $A_\pm$ the areas where $w\ge0$ and
$w<0$, and $\overline w=A^{-1}\int w\,dA$,
$\overline w_\pm=A_\pm^{-1}\int_\pm w\,dA$ the averages, the descriptors of
Politzer and Murray are
\begin{gather}
 \sigma_\pm^2=\frac1{A_\pm}\int_\pm(w-\overline w_\pm)^2\,dA,\qquad
 \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^2=\sigma_+^2+\sigma_-^2,\qquad
 \nu=\frac{\sigma_+^2\sigma_-^2}{(\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^2)^2},\\
 \Pi=\frac1A\int|w-\overline w|\,dA,\qquad
 \mathrm{MPI}=\frac1A\int|w|\,dA .
\end{gather}
For the electrostatic potential ($w=V$) the nonpolar area, where
$|V|\le10$ kcal/mol, is reported too. Local minima (maxima) on the surface
are vertices whose value is below (above) the values of their neighbours
and of the neighbours of those, as in Multiwfn.

For a field that is positive everywhere, such as $\bar I$, the whole
surface counts as positive. Multiwfn evaluates the local electron
attachment energy on the isosurface $\rho=0.004$ by default rather than
$0.001$; with chem-kumo the isovalue must be chosen explicitly.

\subsection{Comparison with Multiwfn}
Multiwfn's quantitative surface analysis (Lu and Chen, J.\ Mol.\ Graph.\
Model.\ 38, 314 (2012)) also triangulates by marching tetrahedra, but it
places the vertices by three bisections and a linear interpolation, merges
vertices closer than $h/2$, and gives each triangle the mean of its vertex
values. Both discretizations converge to the same limit as $h\to0$: at
$h=0.05$ bohr the statistics of water agree to about $3\times10^{-5}$
relative.

\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}

The test suite checks each part of the mathematics above by an independent
route; it establishes agreement on the tested FCHK files, not support for
every Gaussian basis or method.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Derivatives.} AO, MO and density derivatives are compared with
 centred finite differences of independently computed values and
 gradients. Finite differences are used only in tests.
\item \emph{Contractions.} AO-P results are compared with sums over the
 occupied orbitals (density, gradient, Hessian and $\tau$), AO-MO with
 AO-P, blocked with pointwise evaluation, screened with exact
 contractions, and the shell cutoff with its bound.
\item \emph{Gaussian.} Densities and signed orbital amplitudes are compared
 with Cube files written by Gaussian~09 \texttt{cubegen}.
\item \emph{Multiwfn.} Reference Cube files of Multiwfn are used for ELF,
 LOL, $s$, $\operatorname{sign}(\lambda_2)\rho$, IRI, DORI, the spin
 polarization, the energy and information densities, the electrostatic
 potential, $\bar I$, $\mathrm{EA}_L$, $E_{\mathrm{att}}$, $\chi_L$ and
 $\eta_L$, for closed- and open-shell molecules; the surface statistics are
 compared with Multiwfn for the electrostatic potential, $\bar I$,
 $\mathrm{EA}_L$ and $E_{\mathrm{att}}$.
\item \emph{Electrostatic potential.} Besides Multiwfn, it is checked
 against the closed form for densities of s functions, against the
 multipole expansion of the analytic AO moments far from the molecule,
 against Poisson's equation $\nabla^2V_{\mathrm{el}}=4\pi\rho$ by finite
 differences, and its far-field approximations against their error bounds.
 The Boys function is checked against quadrature.
\item \emph{Formulas.} The derived fields are checked against their
 definitions, the Pauli term against its orbital form, and $\bar I$ and the
 virtual-orbital fields against their orbital sums.
\item \emph{Surfaces.} Triangulation and statistics are checked on a
 sphere and an octahedron with closed-form answers.
\item \emph{Grids and points.} Lines and planes are checked against their
 construction rules, and evaluation at arbitrary points against grid
 evaluation at the same points.
\item \emph{Threads.} Results with several OpenMP threads are required to
 be identical, bit for bit, to those with one thread.
\end{itemize}

The optional benchmark programs time AO evaluation, the contractions and
the field evaluations. Each timing is the minimum of repeated wall-clock
times after a warm-up run; timings are measurements, never pass or fail
criteria.

\section{Limits and intended use}
chem-kumo evaluates the fields defined by the density matrices and orbitals
of the supplied FCHK file. It performs no SCF calculation, orbital
optimization, response calculation or differentiation with respect to the
nuclear positions. Its derivatives are analytic for the given Gaussian
basis; sampling on grids and floating-point arithmetic bring their usual
limitations. The blocked evaluators hold the AOs that survive screening in
dense matrices of fixed-size blocks, while the results for all points are
kept in memory.
